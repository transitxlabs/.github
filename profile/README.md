# TransitX Labs Inc.

TransitX explores multimodal mobility through research, design studies and simulation. Its public work so far is five open analyses and demonstrations on public transit data; one finds that removing Edmonton's Capital Line LRT would cut the average resident's 45-minute reach by about 6 percent. Work may consider land, air and water transport concepts. Public material is not evidence of a certified vehicle, flight operation or safety approval.

## Open by design

**We build in the open.** TransitX Labs Inc. publishes its tools, methods and learning materials under open licences (usually Apache-2.0 for code and CC BY 4.0 for written material), so public-interest teams can use them, check how they work and adapt them freely. Open work is easier to trust, because anyone can see exactly how a result is produced.

**Our own work, and only ours.** Everything we publish here is our own, built from public data and synthetic examples. It never includes client data, client projects, or anyone else's confidential information or intellectual property.

## Public projects

- [transit-access-audit](https://github.com/transitxlabs/transit-access-audit): accessibility, service and equity audits for Canadian GTFS transit feeds.
- [edmonton-network-lab](https://github.com/transitxlabs/edmonton-network-lab): how many people Edmonton residents can reach by scheduled transit, and how much each route's removal costs; the Capital Line LRT costs the most.
- [transit-safety-lens](https://github.com/transitxlabs/transit-safety-lens): checks of published US National Transit Database safety records, and how many reported rate changes stand out under a stated model.
- [checked-here](https://github.com/transitxlabs/checked-here): a demonstration of a dated station board of staff-checked safety actions, with synthetic data.
- [fleet-rightsizing](https://github.com/transitxlabs/fleet-rightsizing): a public-data fleet right-sizing demonstration with conservative light-duty screening and sourced factors.
